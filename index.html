The Closing Process, Step by Step

Most residential transactions in New York follow the same general path. Here is what to expect when you work with our office.

  1. Accepted Offer

    Your offer is accepted, the brokers circulate a deal sheet, and each side brings in its attorney. This is the point where you should call us, if you have not already.

  2. Contract of Sale

    The seller's attorney drafts the contract. The buyer's attorney negotiates its terms. Only after both sides sign does the down payment go into escrow.

  3. Due Diligence & Financing

    The inspection, the title search, and the mortgage all move at once. Your attorney keeps track of the deadlines and clears whatever the title report turns up.

  4. Closing Day

    You sign, the funds move, and the keys change hands. Before any of that happens, your attorney has already checked every figure on the closing statement.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need an attorney to buy or sell a home in New York?

In New York, the contract of sale is customarily prepared and negotiated by attorneys, and both sides ordinarily come to the closing with counsel. Your attorney is the one person in the deal whose only job is protecting you. Everyone else gets paid when the deal closes, whether or not it closes well for you.

When should I contact an attorney?

Ideally the day your offer is accepted, and always before you sign anything. Fixing a contract is easy before the signatures and expensive after.

What does a real estate attorney actually do?

Your attorney prepares or reviews the contract of sale and negotiates its terms. After that comes the quieter work: watching the contingency deadlines, clearing title issues, and coordinating with the lender and the other side's counsel. At the closing itself, your attorney checks every document and every figure before you sign.

How long does a closing take?

Most residential closings on Long Island run roughly 60 to 90 days from a signed contract. The mortgage and the title work usually set the pace. We keep the file moving and tell you where things stand as they happen.

What should I bring to my consultation?

Anything you have received so far: the deal sheet, a proposed contract, your lease, or letters from the other side. Nothing in writing yet? That is fine. Call anyway and we will tell you what usually comes next.
