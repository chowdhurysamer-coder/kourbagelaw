The Closing Process, Step by Step

Most residential transactions in New York follow the same general path. Here is what to expect when you work with our office.

  1. Accepted Offer

    Once your offer is accepted (or you accept an offer on your home), the brokers prepare a deal sheet and the attorneys are brought in.

  2. Contract of Sale

    The seller's attorney prepares the contract; the buyer's attorney reviews and negotiates its terms before anything is signed and the down payment is placed in escrow.

  3. Due Diligence & Financing

    Inspections, the title search, and the mortgage process move forward while your attorney tracks contingencies and clears any title issues.

  4. Closing Day

    Documents are signed, funds are disbursed, and the deed and keys change hands — with your attorney at the table to review every figure.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need an attorney to buy or sell a home in New York?

In New York, attorneys customarily prepare and negotiate the contract of sale and represent both sides at closing. Having your own attorney means someone is reviewing the terms, protecting your deposit, and looking out for your interests alone at every stage of the transaction.

When should I contact an attorney?

As early as possible — ideally as soon as you have an accepted offer, and before you sign anything. Early review of the contract is the best chance to address problems while they are still easy to fix.

What does a real estate attorney actually do?

Your attorney prepares or reviews the contract of sale, negotiates its terms, monitors contingencies, works with the title company to clear any issues, coordinates with your lender and the other side's counsel, and represents you at the closing table — reviewing every document and figure before you sign.

How long does a closing take?

Every transaction is different, but most residential closings on Long Island take roughly 60 to 90 days from a signed contract, depending on financing, title, and the parties' circumstances. We keep your transaction moving and keep you informed along the way.

What should I bring to my consultation?

Anything you have received so far — the deal sheet, a proposed contract, your lease, or correspondence from brokers or the other party. If you have nothing in writing yet, that is fine too; call us and we will tell you what to expect.
